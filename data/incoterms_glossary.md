# Incoterms 2020 — Quick Reference (Meridian Intermodal Logistics)

This glossary explains the Incoterms rules Meridian quotes most often to customers. Incoterms allocate
cost, risk, and insurance responsibility between seller and buyer at a defined transfer point — they do
not determine who pays duties unless stated.

## EXW — Ex Works
Seller makes goods available at their own premises. Buyer takes on all risk, transport, export clearance,
and insurance from that point. Lowest seller obligation; highest buyer exposure.

## FOB — Free On Board (sea/inland waterway only)
Seller clears export and delivers goods on board the vessel at the named port of loading. Risk transfers
to the buyer once the goods are on board. Buyer arranges and pays for main carriage and insurance.

## CIF — Cost, Insurance and Freight (sea/inland waterway only)
Seller pays for carriage and a minimum level of marine insurance to the named port of destination. Risk
still transfers at the port of loading (same as FOB), but the seller has a cost obligation beyond that
point. Frequently confused with "seller bears risk to destination" — it does not.

## CPT — Carriage Paid To
Seller pays for carriage to the named destination (any mode). Risk transfers when goods are handed to
the first carrier, not at destination.

## DAP — Delivered At Place
Seller bears all costs and risk until goods arrive at the named destination, ready for unloading. Buyer
handles import clearance and duties. Common for Meridian's door-to-door intermodal quotes.

## DDP — Delivered Duty Paid
Maximum seller obligation: seller delivers, cleared for import, duties paid, ready for unloading at
destination. Meridian only quotes DDP for customers with an existing bonded-import setup on file.

## Internal note
Sales should never quote CIF when the customer's real intent is "delivered and insured to my warehouse" —
that is a DAP or DDP conversation. Misquoting CIF as door-to-door coverage is the single most common
Incoterms error in new-customer onboarding.
